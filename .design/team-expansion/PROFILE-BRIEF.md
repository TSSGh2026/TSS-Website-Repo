# What to send us for your profile

*Forwardable brief for Ahalya, Sreepathy and Raayeed. The fields below are exactly
the slots `SpecialistEntry` renders in `client/src/pages/team.tsx` — nothing more,
because anything else has nowhere to go on the page.*

---

## The message to send them

Hi — so glad you're up for this.

Your profile goes on the Story Shapers site, on the team page, under the three of
us. It's a deliberately sparse format: a photograph, what you do, your name, and
one line. That's it. The restraint is the point — the page is quiet on purpose,
and short entries read as confidence.

Four things, and it should take you twenty minutes.

**1. Your name**
Exactly as you want it published.

**2. Your discipline — a short label**
Two to four words, the craft rather than the job title. It's the first thing read
in your row, set in wide-tracked capitals above your name — on this page the craft
leads and the name follows.
*e.g. "Photography" · "Motion and film" · "Design and art direction"*

Keep it under about 30 characters. Nothing breaks past that, it just stops being
a label and starts being a sentence.

One request across the three of you: don't all send "X and Y". Three rows reading
"something and something" in a column is a pattern the eye catches, and it makes
considered choices look like a template. If your craft is honestly one word, send
one word.

**3. One line on what changes when you're on it — about 20 words**
Not a bio, not a career history, no years of experience. What's different about
the work because you did it. Present tense.
*e.g. "Turns written positioning into a visual system that holds up across every
format a brand has to live in."*

Hard ceiling is about 25 words — the page cuts it off at three lines, so anything
past that simply won't be read.

**4. A photograph**
This is the one that goes wrong most often, so it's worth two minutes of care:

- **Portrait orientation** — taller than it is wide. Not landscape. The frame on
  the page is 4:5.
- **At least 2000px on the long edge.** The existing three are 3000×4500. Please
  don't pull something off LinkedIn or Instagram — those come down at around
  400px and will look visibly broken next to the others.
- **Head in the top third of the frame,** with a little room above your head.
  Don't centre yourself — the site crops these, and a centred portrait crops to a
  torso.
- **Shot somewhere real** — a room, a window, a street. The current three are
  environmental rather than studio, and a white-backdrop headshot will sit oddly
  against them.
- **Not:** a group photo you've cropped yourself out of, a tight phone selfie, or
  anything heavily filtered.

Send it as a single message or email with the photo attached at full size —
please don't paste it into a document, it compresses.

## One optional extra

**Brands you've worked with, up to six.** These don't appear on the page (see
below), but they help us place you and they matter if we later build you a fuller
page. Only ones you can name publicly — if you're unsure about a client, leave it
out.

No LinkedIn, no links, no handles. The entry is the four things above and nothing
else.

## Why these fields and no others

The specialist entry renders four things: photograph, discipline label, name, one
line. Deliberately **not** requested, because nothing consumes it:

- A full bio or career history
- Case studies
- Testimonials
- Stats or numbers

**Why no client list on the page.** A brands line is the heaviest credibility
element on the founders' cards and the main thing giving them their authority.
Granting it to the specialist tier would pull the two tiers level and undo the
hierarchy the section exists to state. If someone's client list is strong enough
to need publishing, that's an argument for giving them a full card, not for
thickening this one.

Those larger fields only become relevant if the decision changes to giving
specialists full `/{slug}` portfolio pages — a much bigger ask of them, since the
founders' records carry hero, brands, stats, case studies, testimonials, about and
work-with-me blocks.

## When the copy comes back

Expect at least one of the three to send a LinkedIn summary regardless of what
was asked. Rewriting it into the two-move shape is a five-minute job and better
than asking twice.

Two things to check before their copy goes in the file:

- The discipline labels, read as a set. If all three come back as "X and Y",
  shorten one.
- The one-liners, read as a set. The three founders' lines are 17, 21 and 32
  words and vary in shape; three specialist lines all built verb-first at the
  same length will read as filled-in rather than written.
